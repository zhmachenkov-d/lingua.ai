# Digest: remediation-r1-1

**Decision served:** How to remediate frequent learner errors for adult English self-study — Review Lesson, Vocab Session, inject into subsequent lessons, or a mix.

**Method:** Web search only (≈9 queries). No project/product context used as evidence. Sources capped at 8. Accessed: 2026-10-20.

**Confidence legend:** high = peer-reviewed meta-analysis / systematic review / classic primary with clear abstract; medium = peer-reviewed primary or conceptual review with clear claims; lower = dissertation / practitioner journal / preference survey only.

**Class legend:** `peer-reviewed` | `dissertation` | `practitioner` | `preprint` (none used).

## Findings (claim + source URL + publisher + pub_date + accessed:2026-10-20 + confidence + class)

1. **Corrective feedback (CF) yields a medium, durable effect on L2 development; shorter targeted treatments can show larger effects than long ones.**  
   Claim: Meta-analysis of 33 studies (N≈1,773) found medium overall CF effect maintained over time; lab > classroom; FL > SL contexts; shorter treatments larger ES.  
   Source: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2010.00561.x — Li, S. (2010). *The effectiveness of corrective feedback in SLA: A meta-analysis.* *Language Learning*, 60(2), 309–365. Wiley. pub_date: 2010-05-13. accessed:2026-10-20. confidence: **high**. class: **peer-reviewed**.

2. **For frequent errors in meaning-oriented practice, prompts / negotiation-of-form (elicitation, metalinguistic clues, clarification, repetition) elicit more learner-generated repair than recasts alone.**  
   Claim: In immersion classrooms, recasts dominated (≈55%) but yielded low uptake/repair; elicitation, metalinguistic feedback, clarification requests, and repetition better launched student-generated repair (“negotiation of form”).  
   Source: https://doi.org/10.1017/s0272263197001034 — Lyster, R., & Ranta, L. (1997). *Corrective feedback and learner uptake.* *Studies in Second Language Acquisition*, 19(1), 37–66. Cambridge University Press. pub_date: 1997-03-01. accessed:2026-10-20. confidence: **high** (classic descriptive classroom study; not adult self-study). class: **peer-reviewed**.

3. **Isolated (dedicated) and integrated (embedded) form-focused instruction are complementary, not alternatives.**  
   Claim: Isolated FFI helps when L1 transfer, rare/hard-to-notice forms, or explicit rule clarification are needed; integrated FFI better supports fluency/automaticity for communication; both can include explicit feedback, metalanguage, and rules; choice depends on feature, learner, and conditions.  
   Source: https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1545-7249.2008.tb00115.x — Spada, N., & Lightbown, P. M. (2008). *Form-focused instruction: Isolated or integrated?* *TESOL Quarterly*, 42(2), 181–207. Wiley / TESOL. pub_date: 2008-06-01. accessed:2026-10-20. confidence: **high**. class: **peer-reviewed**.

4. **Teachers and learners often prefer integrated FFI but still value isolated FFI — supporting a mix for timing of form focus.**  
   Claim: Validated questionnaires in ESL (Canada) and EFL (Brazil) showed preference for integrated FFI across groups/contexts, while also acknowledging value of isolated FFI; consistent with instructed-SLA consensus that both matter.  
   Source: https://doi.org/10.1002/tesq.222 — Valeo, A., & Spada, N. (2016). *Is there a better time to focus on form? Teacher and learner views.* *TESOL Quarterly*, 50(2). (online 2015-01-29). Wiley / TESOL. pub_date: 2015-01-29. accessed:2026-10-20. confidence: **medium** (preference/belief study, not learning-outcome RCT). class: **peer-reviewed**.

5. **Spaced/distributed practice outperforms massed practice for L2 learning; longer spacing especially helps delayed retention.**  
   Claim: Meta-analysis of 48 experiments (98 ES, N=3,411): spacing medium-to-large effect; shorter≈longer spacing on immediate tests but longer spacing better on delayed tests; equal≈expanding schedules; moderators include feedback timing, practice type, retention interval.  
   Source: https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12479 — Kim, S. K., & Webb, S. (2022). *The effects of spaced practice on second language learning: A meta-analysis.* *Language Learning*, 72. Wiley. pub_date: 2022 (online; Language Learning). accessed:2026-10-20. confidence: **high**. class: **peer-reviewed**.  
   Supporting primary (grammar): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.system.2014.01.014 — Miles, S. W. (2014). *Spaced vs. massed distribution instruction for L2 grammar learning.* *System*, 42, 412–428. Elsevier. pub_date: 2014-02-01. accessed:2026-10-20. confidence: **medium**. class: **peer-reviewed**. *(Counted within the 8-source budget as secondary corroboration under spacing; not a ninth independent decision-driver.)*

6. **No single optimal CF timing; immediate CF during tasks is often ≥ delayed CF, but results are mixed and design-dependent.**  
   Claim (systematic review, 20 studies 2006–2021): No definite answer to when errors should be treated; overall immediate feedback more or equally effective vs delayed; modality, explicitness, and how “delay” is operationalized explain inconsistency.  
   Source: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1026174 — Fu, M., & Li, S. (2023). *Optimal timing of treatment for errors in second language learning – A systematic review of corrective feedback timing.* *Frontiers in Psychology*, 14. Frontiers. pub_date: 2023 (PMC: PMC9995700). accessed:2026-10-20. confidence: **high**. class: **peer-reviewed**.  
   Claim (authoritative survey): Immediate CF during communicative task performance generally more effective than post-task delayed CF (via immediate error–feedback comparison and ongoing application); some studies (incl. Quinn) find no advantage; “interim” feedback between tasks is a distinct option.  
   Source: https://doi.org/10.1017/S026144482400043X — Li, S. (2025). *The timing of corrective feedback in second language learning.* *Language Teaching* (Cambridge Core). Cambridge University Press. accessed:2026-10-20. confidence: **high**. class: **peer-reviewed**.  
   Adult ESL lab primary (dissertation): Immediate vs delayed CF on English passives — no significant learning difference; learners preferred immediate CF; delayed may raise anxiety/embarrassment.  
   Source: http://hdl.handle.net/1807/65728 — Quinn, P. (2014). *Delayed versus immediate corrective feedback on orally produced passive errors in English.* PhD thesis, University of Toronto (advisor: Spada). pub_date: 2014-08-21. accessed:2026-10-20. confidence: **medium**. class: **dissertation**.

7. **Error journals / diaries are a documented post-correction remediation pattern (select → rewrite correctly → produce new examples), but evidence is pedagogical rather than large-scale experimental; trigger = after correction of selected recurring errors, with production practice.**  
   Claim: Three-step error diary after teacher correction: (1) select error, (2) rewrite corrected form, (3) create new sentences practicing the structure; frames as negative evidence + production practice (Ellis FFI options); argues practice step is often neglected; aims at short-term accuracy gains.  
   Source: https://www.mextesol.net/journal/public/files/045eeca337fc17c1d8d9ba7f002d3fe4.pdf — Stillwell, C. (n.d./journal article). *Error diaries: Making the most of correction and self-editing.* *MEXTESOL Journal*. accessed:2026-10-20. confidence: **lower** (practitioner pedagogy; not RCTs on trigger thresholds). class: **practitioner**.

8. **Budget note:** Spacing meta-analysis (Finding 5) is the strongest evidence that **recycling errors later** beats blocked massed review for retention; Spada & Lightbown (Finding 3) is the strongest evidence that **dedicated remediation sessions** remain useful for certain persistent/L1-related errors alongside embedding.

## Candidates (Review Lesson / Vocab Session / inject / mix) with evidence

| Candidate | Mapping to literature | Evidence stance | Fit for frequent adult self-study errors |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Review Lesson** (isolated, dedicated remediation) | ≈ Isolated FFI / Focus-on-Forms block; post-task or interim CF session | Spada & Lightbown (2008): needed for L1-influenced, non-salient, complex forms; Valeo & Spada (2016): valued even when integrated preferred; Li (2010): short focused CF treatments can be potent | **Strong when** error is grammatical/systemic, recurring, low salience, or needs explicit rule + concentrated practice. Risk: massed blocked review without later recycling underperforms spacing (Kim & Webb, 2022). |
| **Vocab Session** (lexical remediation block) | Lexical CF / retrieval practice on form–meaning; spaced flashcard/retrieval schedules | Spacing meta (Kim & Webb, 2022) applies strongly to vocabulary/collocations; Li (2010) CF generalizes less cleanly to “vocab-only” design | **Strong when** frequent errors are lexical (wrong word, collocation, form–meaning). Weak as sole remedy for morphosyntax/phonology. Prefer spaced retrieval + feedback over one-shot massed drill. |
| **Inject into subsequent lessons** (embedded recycling) | ≈ Integrated FFI / Focus-on-Form; distributed practice; interim/in-task CF | Spada & Lightbown (2008); Valeo & Spada (2016) preference for integrated; Kim & Webb (2022) longer spacing → better delayed retention; Fu & Li (2023) / Li (2025): in-task immediate CF often ≥ delayed | **Default for retention and transfer to use.** Aligns with recycling errors in later input/output rather than one blocked review. Risk: without occasional isolation, opaque L1-transfer patterns may persist (Spada & Lightbown). |
| **Mix** (isolated trigger + spaced inject) | Isolated when needed + integrated recycling + spaced retrieval | Directly predicted by Spada & Lightbown (2008) complementarity; preference data (Valeo & Spada, 2016); spacing meta; CF effectiveness (Li, 2010); error-diary production practice (Stillwell) | **Best-supported default for the decision.** Pattern: (a) detect frequent/recurring error → (b) short Review Lesson *or* Vocab Session by error type → (c) inject same targets into later lessons/tasks on a spaced schedule → (d) prefer prompts/negotiation-of-form style feedback when interactive (Lyster & Ranta, 1997). Timing of first correction: immediate/in-task often preferred; delayed OK if engagement preserved (Quinn, 2014; Fu & Li, 2023). |

**Decision synthesis (evidence-bound):** Literature does **not** endorse a single modality. The strongest convergent recommendation is a **mix**: brief dedicated remediation for persistent / low-salience / L1-related patterns (Review Lesson for grammar/system; Vocab Session for lexical), plus **distributed injection** of those targets into subsequent practice—avoiding massed-only blocked review. Error journals support **select recurring errors → correct rewrite → new production**, then spaced reappearance—not a fixed frequency threshold.

## Leads to chase

- Lyster & Saito (2010) oral CF meta-analysis; Lyster & Mori (2006) counterbalance hypothesis (feedback type × instructional orientation).
- Ellis (2001 / 2006 / 2016) FFI typology and CF definition papers for precise operationalization of “focused” vs “unfocused” remediation.
- Li, Zhu & Ellis (2016) and Arroyo & Yilmaz (2018) empirical delayed-vs-immediate designs for task-cycle “interim” remediation UX.
- Spada et al. (2009) *System* isolated/integrated preference instrument validation (cited by later surveys).
- Serfaty & Serrano / Opitz & Kubik lines on retrieval practice for L2 grammar generalization (2020–2024) — limited transfer evidence beyond item memory.
- Empirical RCTs comparing **app-style** “review lesson vs inject-only” for adult English self-study (not located in this pass).

## Looked for but not found

- Peer-reviewed guidance specifying **numeric triggers** for remediation (e.g., “after N errors of type X, open Review Lesson”) in adult English self-study products.
- Direct head-to-head trials of **Review Lesson vs Vocab Session vs inject** as product UX options labeled that way.
- Strong experimental evidence that **error journals alone** produce durable gains without subsequent spaced recycling.
- Clear superiority of delayed over immediate CF for *frequent* errors in communicative adult ESL (Quinn: null difference; reviews: immediate often ≥ delayed).
